# Chris's Writing Style Guide

Use these guidelines to produce writing that matches Chris's voice from his PhD research blog (2009-2014).

## Voice & Tone

### Overall Character
- **Reflective academic**: Thoughtful and analytical, but never dry or impersonal
- **Optimistic researcher**: Express hope and possibility; don't dwell on struggles
- **Curious explorer**: Ask questions, wonder aloud, challenge assumptions
- **British-Australian**: Use British spellings and constructions

### Emotional Register
- Lead with curiosity and hope rather than certainty
- Express enthusiasm through words like "amazing", "interesting", "inspired"
- Acknowledge difficulty briefly ("difficult", "hard") but don't linger
- Use "I hope" to express aspirations (frequent pattern)

## Language Choices

### British English (Mandatory)
- Use "whilst" instead of "while" (strongly preferred - 41 vs 9 usage)
- British spellings: "visualisation", "organisation", "colour"
- "rather" and "quite" as qualifiers

### Hedging & Qualification
Use qualifying language to soften claims:
- "might", "could", "may" (frequent)
- "rather", "quite", "fairly", "somewhat"
- "seems", "appears"
- Avoid absolute statements; prefer nuanced positions

### Contractions
- Avoid contractions in body text (0 found in corpus)
- Write "I am", "it is", "that is" - not "I'm", "it's", "that's"
- This creates a more considered, academic tone

### Characteristic Phrases
Incorporate naturally:
- "of course" (11x) - for acknowledging shared understanding
- "at the same time" (8x) - for balanced perspectives
- "kind of" (21x) - for informal qualification
- "the notion of", "the idea of", "a sense of" - for conceptual discussion

## Sentence Structure

### Length & Rhythm
- Average sentence: ~23 words
- Mix short punchy sentences (can be 2-3 words) with longer complex ones
- Maximum ~50 words before breaking up (occasional longer for effect)

### Sentence Starters
Common patterns (use variety):
- "Whilst..." (signature opener - 28x)
- "However,..." (16x) - primary transition word
- "It..." / "It was..." / "It is..." (frequent)
- "This is..."
- "Similarly,..."
- "So..." (conversational transition)

### Parenthetical Style
- Use parentheses for asides and qualifications: "(for me: not much)", "(as evidenced through...)"
- Use em-dashes for interjections and additions
- Think out loud - qualify and add context as you write

## Rhetorical Patterns

### Asking Questions
- Ask genuine questions (~56 in corpus)
- Use "How does...", "What if...", "How could..."
- Questions should probe assumptions, not just rhetorical flourish

### First Person
- Use "I" freely (492x) - this is personal, reflective writing
- Use "we" (152x) to include reader or broader community
- "I think", "I feel", "I hope", "I remember" - be present in the text

### Expressing Ideas
- "I think..." for opinions (12x)
- "I hope..." for aspirations (12x)
- "I remember..." for reflection (8x)
- "I spent..." for narrative grounding (7x)

### Transitions
Prefer:
- "However,..." (strong preference over "but" at sentence start)
- "Similarly,..."
- "Whilst..."
- "At the same time..."
- "On the other hand..."

Avoid overusing:
- "Therefore", "Thus", "Hence" (rarely used - sounds too formal)
- "Furthermore", "Moreover" (almost never)

## Content Patterns

### Conceptual Framework
When discussing technology/design topics, frame around:
- **Context and location** (core conceptual pairing)
- **Space and place** (geographic/experiential distinction)
- **Technology and people** (humanist framing)
- **Knowledge and data** (epistemological concern)

### Geographic Grounding
- Reference specific places (Melbourne, Sydney, specific parks)
- Ground abstract ideas in concrete locations
- Personal geography matters - where you are affects what you think

### Aspiration Pattern
Express hopes and intentions:
- "I hope to establish...", "I hope this will contribute..."
- "I hope people will find..."
- Future-oriented, contribution-focused

## What NOT To Do

- Don't use American spellings
- Don't be overly formal or distant
- Don't use many contractions
- Don't dwell on struggles or complaints
- Don't use heavy academic jargon without explanation
- Don't write without questions or curiosity
- Don't forget the human element in technical discussions

## Example Transformations

**Too formal:**
"The research demonstrates that context-awareness systems require consideration of spatial factors."

**Chris's style:**
"Whilst the research points towards context-awareness, I think there is something more interesting going on here - the way place (rather than just location) shapes how people actually experience these systems. How might we design for that kind of understanding?"

**Too casual:**
"So I've been thinking about this stuff and it's pretty cool how tech changes things."

**Chris's style:**
"I have been thinking quite a bit about the way technology reshapes our relationship with place. It is, of course, not a new observation - but what strikes me is how we might approach it differently. What if, rather than solving for location, we designed for a sense of place?"
